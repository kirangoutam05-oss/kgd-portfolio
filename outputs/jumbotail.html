Jumbotail

A year designing the own-brand shelf for a B2B wholesale marketplace — the packaging, the shop front and the campaigns that carry it. Jumbofarms is the staples brand, with Vedha and Crispy Corner alongside it.

Packaging DesignDesign SystemsBrand ArchitectureRetailCampaign
ClientJumbotail Technologies
RoleIn-house designer
DatesJun 2024 — Sep 2025
Context

A white-label brand that exists to widen somebody else’s margin.

Jumbofarms is Jumbotail’s own white-label brand, sold exclusively on Jumbotail to B2B kirana customers. It carries the same quality as the branded goods sitting beside it on the marketplace, and its whole reason for existing is that it gives the shopkeeper buying it a better margin than those brands do.

That makes the packaging the entire argument. There is no store frontage doing the persuading and no advertising behind it; the sack on the pallet is the brand, and it has to work for a shopkeeper buying twenty-six kilos at a time as well as for the household the shopkeeper sells it on to. A white label that looks like a compromise does not get a second order, however good the margin is.

It started small — a simple dal — and grew into the range documented here: rice, dals, flour, spices and oil, plus the brands alongside it.

The role

In-house, which means the brief is yours to find.

As the in-house designer I was responsible for the work before the artwork: user research, understanding what the kirana customer actually needed, and understanding what the packaging itself was capable of — substrate, format, print process and what a converter could hold to at volume.

A staples brand is also a regulated one. Every pack had to be designed and developed against FSSAI and food safety norms, which govern what must appear, at what size, and where. On a 26 kg sack that is a layout constraint; on a ₹10 sachet it is most of the front of pack.

The shape of it

Three brands, two shelves, one system underneath.

Jumbofarms carries the staples and behaves like a house brand — one oval mark, one set of quality roundels, one way of photographing a bowl of the raw product. Within it, each range solves its own problem: nine grades of basmati separated by colour alone, a cumin bag that changes by a ribbon, a mustard oil that had to survive being both a bottle and a pouch.

Jumbofarms is the one that behaves like a brand rather than a label: nine ranges, a shared mark, and a retail kit that puts it on the front of the shop. Vedha and Crispy Corner sit outside it, because a pooja ghee and a ₹10 namkeen are not staples and should not look like them. Vedha even shares a bottle with the mustard oil — the label is the only thing keeping them apart.

Alongside the packaging sat everything that carries it: a retail layer of fascia and danglers, a membership programme, a year of social, an export brochure and the campaigns that ran on the shelf.
